# Rivu — listing copy and outreach

Working document. Every claim here is checked against what the app actually
does; if a feature changes, change the copy in the same commit.

---

## 1. App Store listing

### Before you paste anything: get the numbers

Partner Dashboard → Apps → Rivu → Analytics. Three figures decide which half of
this document matters:

| Impressions | Installs | What it means | Where to spend time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near zero | Near zero | Nobody finds you | Keywords, below |
| Healthy | Near zero | They find you and leave | Listing page + reviews |

Rewriting copy when the problem is that you have no impressions is wasted
effort, and vice versa.

### App name

Shopify weights the app name heavily in search, and merchants search for what
they want, not for brand names nobody knows yet.

```
Rivu Product Reviews & UGC
```

Reasoning: "Product Reviews" is the phrase merchants actually type. "UGC" picks
up photo/video-review searches. The brand stays first so it still reads as a
name.

### Tagline (short description)

```
Photo & video reviews, review request emails, and a widget that matches your theme
```

Three concrete things, no adjectives. "Matches your theme" is the wedge —
merchants complain constantly that review widgets look bolted on.

### Long description opening

The first two lines are all most merchants read. Lead with the switch, because
almost everyone you can win is already using something else.

```
Already using Judge.me, Loox, Stamped or Yotpo? Export your reviews as CSV and
import them into Rivu in one step — original dates and ratings kept, nothing
retyped, and you can see exactly what will import before it does.

Rivu collects reviews with photos and video, emails your customers to ask for
them after they order, and shows the results in a widget you can actually make
look like your store.
```

### Feature bullets

Only claims the app can back up today:

- **Photo and video reviews** — collected on your product page, no redirect
- **Automatic review request emails** — sent after an order, with your branding
- **Eight widget layouts** — plus full HTML/CSS control on Pro
- **Rich snippets** — star ratings in Google results, on by default
- **QR codes** — collect reviews from packaging or in person
- **One-step migration** — Judge.me, Loox, Stamped, Yotpo, or any CSV
- **Free plan** — no trial clock

Do **not** list: Top Reviewer streak badges (removed).

### Search terms

Fill every available slot; these are the phrases merchants type:

```
product reviews, photo reviews, video reviews, review app, customer reviews,
review widget, judge.me alternative, loox alternative, ugc, star ratings,
google rich snippets, review request email, qr code reviews
```

The `X alternative` terms matter most — someone typing them has already decided
to switch and is choosing between options.

### Screenshots — in this order

The first two are what people actually see; treat the rest as optional.

1. **The widget on a real product page.** Not the admin. Merchants buy the thing
   their customers will see.
2. **The same widget in three different designs, side by side.** This is your
   differentiator and no competitor screenshot shows it.
3. The import preview screen, captioned "Bring your reviews from Judge.me,
   Loox, Stamped or Yotpo".
4. The review request email.
5. The dashboard.

A common mistake is leading with the admin dashboard. Nobody buys a dashboard.

## 2. Getting your first reviews

Your listing has no reviews, which for a *reviews app* is the whole ball game.
Nothing above will convert until this is fixed, and it can only be fixed by
hand.

Target: **ten merchants you have personally helped**. Not a hundred installs.

For each one: install it with them, import their existing reviews yourself,
check the widget looks right on their theme, and follow up a week later. Then —
and only then — ask.

Ask like this, after you have actually helped them:

```
Glad it's working on your store. If you have a minute, a review on the Shopify
App Store would genuinely help me — you'd be one of the first, so it makes a
real difference at this stage. No worries if not.
```

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. It violates Shopify's terms and
can get the app removed.

## 3. Outreach

### Who to contact

Small Shopify stores with **no review widget on their product pages**. You can
see this in ten seconds: open a product page and look. No stars near the title
and no reviews section below means no review app.

These are far better prospects than stores already running Judge.me, because
there is nothing to switch and no data to move.

Do 10 a day. It is slow, unscalable, and the fastest route from zero.

### Cold email / DM — no review app

Short, specific to their store, no pitch deck.

```
Subject: no reviews on your [product name] page

Hi [name],

I was looking at [store] — the [specific product] page has no customer reviews
on it. For most stores that's the single biggest thing holding back conversion,
because nobody wants to be the first to buy.

I build Rivu, a reviews app for Shopify. There's a free plan, so this doesn't
have to cost you anything to try.

Happy to set it up for you myself and make it match your theme — takes about
fifteen minutes and I won't charge you for it. Worth a look?

[your name]
```

Why it works: names a specific problem on *their* store, offers to do the work,
and asks for a small yes.

### Cold email — already using Judge.me or Loox

Migration is the only objection worth addressing here.

```
Subject: switching review apps without losing your reviews

Hi [name],

You're running [Judge.me/Loox] on [store]. Most people stay put because moving
reviews is a pain — you export a CSV and hope for the best.

Rivu imports that export directly. Original dates and ratings kept, and it
shows you exactly what will import before anything happens, so there's no
guessing.

The reason people move: you get full control of how the widget looks, including
your own CSS, so it stops looking like a bolted-on third-party block.

Free plan, and I'll do the migration with you if you want. Interested?

[your name]
```

### Shopify Partners and agencies

One agency can install you across every store they build. Higher effort per
conversation, much higher payoff.

```
Hi [name],

You build Shopify stores for clients — do you have a reviews app you default to?

I build Rivu. The reason it might suit agency work specifically: the review
widget takes your own HTML and CSS on the Pro plan, so you can make it match a
client's design system instead of accepting whatever the app renders.

Happy to give you Pro free on your own store so you can try it properly before
putting it in front of a client.

[your name]
```

Giving agencies free access is worth it. They are a distribution channel, not a
customer.

### Where to post (and how not to get banned)

| Channel | Approach |
|---|---|
| r/shopify, r/ecommerce | Answer review/conversion questions properly. Mention the app only if directly relevant, and disclose that you built it. |
| Shopify Community forums | Same, and these rank in Google for years afterwards. |
| Facebook Shopify groups | Read the rules first — most ban promotion outright but allow "I built this, feedback welcome" posts. |
| LinkedIn | Deprioritise. Store owners are not there in numbers. Keep it for agency/partner contacts only. |

Rule for all of them: be useful ten times before you mention the app once.
Self-promotion in these communities is noticed instantly and permanently.

## 4. What not to do

- Don't buy installs or reviews — it gets apps removed.
- Don't offer discounts in exchange for reviews — same.
- Don't scale outreach until you have ten happy merchants. Bugs found by
  merchant 100 cost more than bugs found by merchant 5, because they arrive as
  public one-star reviews you cannot remove.
- Don't keep posting daily to a channel that has produced nothing. Two weeks of
  data is enough to judge.

## 5. Pricing (updated)

Two tiers. Free is genuinely free: **unlimited reviews**, no trial clock, no
card. Pro is **$14.99/mo**.

The old three-tier structure capped Free at 25 reviews/month and priced Pro
at twice the current figure. Both were wrong for this market:

- Competing free plans carry unlimited reviews, so a 25/month cap meant Rivu
  stopped working exactly when a store started succeeding. Anyone comparing
  the two picked the unlimited one in seconds, and the cap saved nothing —
  text reviews cost almost nothing to store.
- Charging double a $15 competitor that ships more features is not a position
  you can argue from, whatever the listing says.

**Growth has been removed entirely.** It never had a subscriber, so there was
no legacy to carry and no reason to keep a third tier of branching in the code.

### You must update Shopify Managed Pricing

Plans live in the Partner Dashboard, not in this repo. Until you change them
there, the code and the checkout disagree.

1. Partner Dashboard → Apps → Rivu → **Pricing**
2. Set the Pro plan to **$14.99/mo**, keep the 4-day trial
3. **Delete the Growth plan.** It has no subscribers, so nothing is cancelled.
   (Had there been any, deleting would cancel them — hiding it would be the
   move instead.)
4. Confirm the plan is still literally named "Pro": plan detection matches on
   the name, so renaming it to something like "Premium" silently drops paying
   merchants back to Free.

### What stays paid, and why

Video reviews. This is the one limit with a real cost behind it: media is
stored as base64 inside Postgres, so it is the only thing here whose cost
scales with usage. Matching a competitor's "unlimited free video" needs object
storage first — that is the next piece of work, and it is a prerequisite, not
a nice-to-have.
